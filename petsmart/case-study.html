PetSmart redesign

A conceptual digital ecosystem that treats PetSmart as more than a pet-supply store — unifying adoption, commerce, services, and trusted care education.

The problem

Pet retailers often feel like catalogs first and care platforms second. The opportunity was to redesign PetSmart so adoption, shopping, in-store services, and pet-care education read as one coherent experience — warm and trustworthy without becoming cartoonish.

Approach

Four pillars drive information architecture and homepage hierarchy.

01 · Adopt

Emotional entry point

Homepage hero leads with PetSmart Charities and “Find a Pet,” then deepens into pet listings, pet detail, and Charities storytelling.

02 · Shop

Commerce with clarity

Category filters, search, variants, reviews, sticky add-to-cart, multi-step checkout, and favorites — PetSmart-branded, not a template clone.

03 · Services

Bookable care

Grooming, training, and Doggie Day Camp move beyond brochure pages into a multi-step booking flow with store, time, and confirmation.

04 · Learn & Care

Editorial trust

A resource hub with topic filters, full articles, author/date meta, and related reading — useful content, not promo blocks.

Design system

PetSmart red for high-priority actions. Blue for trust and navigation. White and pale-blue environments so brand color can breathe. Plus Jakarta Sans for display, Source Sans 3 for body. Shared chrome, tokens, and reusable cards keep twenty-plus pages consistent.

What’s in the prototype

A clickable ecosystem — not just a homepage mock.

  • Homepage with adoption hero, pathways, ecosystem, featured pets, shop, and resources
  • Shop: categories, filters, search, PDP, cart, multi-step checkout
  • Services: grooming, training, day camp, vet + booking flow
  • Adopt: pet grid, pet detail, PetSmart Charities page
  • Care hub + articles with related content
  • Stores locator + detail, account orders/favorites/appointments

Launch live prototype

Outcome

The redesign reframes PetSmart as a care platform with commerce inside it — not a catalog with services bolted on. The prototype is ready for concept alignment, content replacement, and public hosting as a portfolio case study.
